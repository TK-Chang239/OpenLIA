Lia · TL;DR

We initiate AAPL at Buy with a 12-month price target of $245. Services revenue compounded at 14.1% over the last five years and now contributes 26% of total revenue at 74% gross margin — a structural mix shift that the market has yet to fully capitalize.[1]

Vision Pro remains a ~$3.4B business — small, but a credible runway for spatial computing as content libraries deepen. We model it as ~1% of FY27 revenue, with optionality, not the thesis.[2]

01 — Executive Summary

A maturing hardware franchise compounded by a software-economics tail

Apple is no longer a phone-cycle stock. Through five years of post-pandemic noise, the iPhone installed base expanded from ~1.0B to ~1.46B active devices[3], and the company has methodically converted that base into recurring software-economics revenue: App Store, services, advertising, and financial products.

We see three under-appreciated factors driving a re-rating: (1) Services mix continuing to lift consolidated gross margin into the 47–48% range, (2) a credible AI-on-device thesis as Apple Intelligence matures into a paid tier in late FY26, and (3) capital return remains a floor — buybacks reduced share count by 3.1% in FY25 alone.[4]

Our $245 target reflects 28x NTM EPS, a modest premium to the 5-yr mean of 25x that we view as justified by the higher quality of the marginal earnings dollar.

02 — Investment Thesis

Three pillars that, together, support a Buy

Pillar 01

Services as the structural margin lever

Services now 26% of revenue at 74% GM. Each 100bp of mix shift = ~30bp consolidated GM expansion.

Pillar 02

AI-on-device as a re-rating catalyst

Apple Intelligence shifts from free feature to paid tier in late FY26. Conservative 15% attach on iPhone base = ~$8B high-margin run-rate.

Pillar 03

Capital return as a floor under the multiple

$110B annualized buyback + dividend program. Even at flat earnings, ~3% shrink/yr is meaningful in our terminal.

None of these are individually sufficient for a Buy. Together, they shift the risk/reward favorably — particularly given the cash cushion and a 5-yr mean reversion in iPhone replacement cycles already in our model.

03 — Business Overview

Five revenue lines, one operating system, one customer

Apple Inc. (AAPL) designs and sells consumer electronics, software, and digital services from a single integrated stack. The reportable segments are: iPhone, Mac, iPad, Wearables, Home & Accessories ("WHA"), and Services. Geographically, the largest reporting region is Americas at 42% of FY25 revenue, followed by Europe (25%), Greater China (17%), Japan, and Rest of Asia Pacific.[1]

What's interesting

The company runs a deeply vertical stack — silicon (M-series, A-series), OS, distribution (App Store), payments (Apple Pay), and identity. Each layer is a margin pool and a switching-cost moat. The iPhone is the customer-acquisition engine; everything else is monetization.

What's underwriting the rate?

Three components: App Store (still ~40% of segment), cloud + media subscriptions (Music, TV+, iCloud+, Fitness+), and licensing (notably the Google TAC). The TAC line is a known regulatory overhang — we discuss in Section 11.

06 — Vision Pro Contribution

Small now, optional later — explicitly not the thesis

Vision Pro shipped an estimated ~720k units in FY25 at a blended ASP of ~$3,200, contributing roughly $2.3B of revenue plus an additional ~$1.1B in attached accessories and developer-side App Store revenue. Total: ~$3.4B, or 0.8% of FY25 revenue.[2]

We model Vision Pro at 1.0% of revenue in FY27 and 1.6% in FY28. The unit economics matter less than the platform — visionOS 3 is the first time Apple has a developer-facing AR runtime worth taking seriously, and the cost of being wrong on the thesis is bounded by the small absolute base.

08 — Valuation

$245 target, 28x NTM EPS, triangulated

We triangulate three approaches. (a) NTM P/E: $8.75 × 28x = $245. (b) DCF: 9.0% WACC, 3.5% terminal, services mix re-rating to 32% by FY30 → $251. (c) Sum-of-parts: hardware at 18x, services at 32x → $238. Average rounds to our $245 point estimate.[5]

Why 28x and not the 5-yr mean of 25x?

Three points of premium come from: services mix (+1.5x), AI-on-device optionality (+1.0x), and capital return-driven share count compression (+0.5x). We'd take the multiple back to mean if any one of those legs visibly weakens.

11 — Risks

What would change our call from Buy to Hold

↓ Downside risks
  • TAC ruling: An adverse DOJ outcome on the Google search payments would remove ~$20B of high-margin Services revenue; we'd cut PT to $215.
  • iPhone replacement extension: Cycle moving from 4.0 to 4.4 yrs cuts FY27 unit forecast by ~9%; ~$10 to PT.
  • China demand softening: Greater China is 17% of revenue with above-corporate gross margin. A 10% revenue hit ≈ $7 to PT.
  • App Store regulation: EU DMA compliance margin drag; modeled at 50bp consolidated GM by FY27.
↑ Upside risks
  • AI Pro monetization: Attach above 20% adds ~$3B / yr at services-class margins; ~+$10 to PT.
  • Cycle compression: 3.7-yr replacement on AI-driven utility = +6% FY27 units; +$12 to PT.
  • Buyback acceleration: Cash deployment above $120B/yr meaningfully tightens share count.
  • Vision Pro 2 traction: Asymmetric — small downside, optionality if it lands.
12 — ESG & Governance

Quick scan

Apple is on track for its 2030 carbon-neutral commitment across the value chain — Scope 1 and 2 are effectively neutralized; Scope 3 (supply chain + product use) is the work in progress.[6] Governance: dual-class structure absent, board independence is high, CEO comp is 89% performance-linked. Worker-rights disclosures around supply chain remain the most material ESG risk surface.
